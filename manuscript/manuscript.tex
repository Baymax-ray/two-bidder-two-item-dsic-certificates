\begin{abstract}
Multidimensional incentives couple allocations across reports and across bidders.
For two additive bidders and two items with independent uniform values, we
construct a randomized auction satisfying dominant-strategy incentive
compatibility (DSIC), ex-post individual rationality (IR) and joint feasibility
at every report. Its revenue lies between $0.876514341027$ and
$0.876514355425$. A common capacity-price certificate, a mathematical charge
on allocation used to bound every admissible auction's revenue, gives an
unrestricted upper below $0.8823515854888$. The gap is below $0.005837245$,
hence below $0.01$, and the auction earns more than $99.3384\%$ of optimal
revenue. A global reserve transformation moves both bidders' menus together,
transfers ownership on a region of positive measure, and yields an exact
sixteen-face revenue formula. Its incentive-preservation argument is
independent of the value distribution, and its positive revenue derivative
identifies a profitable variation for the specified seed. The upper
certificate combines conditional bounds under one capacity price, accounting
for the other bidder's virtual value and for overlapping incentive constraints.
Continuous computational certificates separate price reductions from
integration remainders and state their implementation and arithmetic trust
boundaries. The unrestricted optimum and a matching mechanism remain open.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Optimal auction design is completely understood for a single item under the
classical regularity conditions of Myerson~\citep{myerson1981}. With several
items, bidder types become multidimensional and dominant-strategy incentive
compatibility links allocations across all reports. Even the canonical case
of two additive bidders, two items and independent uniform values lacks an
exact optimal mechanism~\citep{sandholm2015,jiang2026duality}.

The difficulty is joint: changing one bidder's menu changes the capacity and
information rents available to the other. A locally profitable price change
can violate supply at a different profile. Conversely, a conditional revenue
bound supports the whole auction only if the same capacity price controls
both bidders. We construct a feasible mechanism and an unrestricted upper
certificate that address these two compatibility requirements directly.

The mechanism applies a common reserve transformation to an explicitly
specified system of three conditional menus. It selects both allocations
at the same transformed profile, preserving joint supply. Nonnegative
allocations and exclusion of zero-valued items at every selected tie preserve
DSIC and individual rationality. The transformation transfers ownership on
a region of positive measure. Its exact revenue includes all sixteen faces
of the transformed distribution, because the reserve creates atoms at zero.

The upper certificate combines a radial revenue identity, a tangent stream
field, conditional screening supports and nonnegative translated incentive
inequalities. Conditional replacements explicitly pay the opposing bidder's
virtual-value excess. Corrections on shared endpoint cells are summed before
taking the common itemwise maximum. This yields a smaller valid capacity
price without imposing a finite-menu restriction on competitors.

The resulting theorem gives an explicit mechanism and certified endpoints
for the full continuous randomized DSIC problem. The incentive-preservation
argument applies to any Borel seed with bounded payments satisfying its stated hypotheses on this report
domain; uniformity is needed for the displayed face revenue formula.
The support identities and shared-cell accounting are analytic tools, while
the chosen menus, fields and quantitative savings are specific to this
instance. We do not claim general convergence or identify an optimal auction.

\subsection{Relation to prior work}
Computational affine-maximizer searches provide transparent deterministic
benchmarks~\citep{sandholm2015}. GemNet constructs fully strategyproof menu
mechanisms through a compatibility transformation~\citep{wang2024gemnet}.
Jiang, Parkes and Wang report approximately $0.876$ revenue for that benchmark
and a strict $50\times50$ continuous uniform upper certificate of $0.8919$
\citep{jiang2026duality}. We improve the printed upper certificate and give an
independently certified revenue enclosure for our explicitly specified auction.
The rounded GemNet report does not establish a ranking against its unrounded
revenue; its mechanism and the external dual array are not replayed here.

Rochet's cyclic-monotonicity characterization turns DSIC into convexity of
truthful utility and identifies allocation vectors with subgradients
\citep{rochet1987}.  Continuous duality for the one-bidder multi-item problem
has been developed through transport and partial-derivative formulations
\citep{daskalakis2017strong,giannakopoulos2018duality}.  These results motivate
integration-by-parts certificates but do not directly enforce item supply
across several bidders.

Multi-bidder dual formulations also predate the present work.  Finite-type
flow-induced virtual values arise in unified Bayesian mechanism-design
duality~\citep{cai2018duality}; Zuo considers both Bayesian and dominant-
strategy formulations~\citep{zuo2017}; and Beckmann-style transport gives a
continuous strong-duality formulation for multi-item, multi-bidder BIC
auctions in interim space~\citep{kolesnikov2022beckmann}.  The latter problem
uses weaker incentive and individual-rationality requirements than the
pointwise DSIC and ex-post IR requirements studied here.

Most directly, Jiang, Parkes, and Wang construct nonnegative, flow-conserving
DSIC deviation kernels, derive continuous weak duality, and lift coarse
uniform-grid certificates to continuous type spaces
\citep{jiang2026duality}.  Our proof shares the same high-level weak-duality
architecture: conservation eliminates payments, the dual object induces
virtual coefficients, and ex-post feasibility bounds virtual surplus
itemwise.  The representation and certification are different.  We use a
signed vector field with a distributional radial source plus a
divergence-free tangent stream correction, and prove its validity directly
against Lipschitz convex utilities.  We do not assert that every such stream
field is generated by a nonnegative deviation kernel, or conversely.  The
contribution is an explicit rational witness for this canonical DSIC instance,
an exact Bernstein certificate for its continuous objective, and explicit
conditional capacity supports that guide feasible two-bidder reallocations.
The convex-mixture concavity of residual value is standard;
the new theorem content is the displayed residual-specific measures, their
full-class slack identities, and the resulting complete primal variations.
We make no first-in-literature priority claim for the general use of convex
potentials, transport, or complementary slackness.

\section{Model and certified bounds}\label{sec:model}

Write a report profile as
\[
 (x,y,z,w)=(v_{11},v_{12},v_{21},v_{22})\in[0,1]^4.
\]
The two bidders are risk-neutral and additive; the two items are indivisible.
The four coordinates are independent and uniform.  A direct mechanism
specifies jointly measurable expected allocation probabilities
\(x_{ij}:[0,1]^4\to[0,1]\) and expected payments
\(p_i:[0,1]^4\to\mathbb R\), with \(p_i\in L^1([0,1]^4)\).
At every report profile, itemwise feasibility requires
\(x_{1j}+x_{2j}\leq1\).  These marginals can be realized by assigning each
item to bidder 1, bidder 2, or the seller with the corresponding probabilities.
DSIC and ex-post IR are required at every type and opponent report, with
utilities averaged over the mechanism's internal randomization.  Let \(\OPT\)
be the supremum of expected revenue over this class.
Our constructed mechanisms are Borel. The upper argument also covers joint
Lebesgue measurability with integrable payments: on every opponent fiber,
DSIC supplies a convex Lipschitz utility and the selected allocation equals
its gradient off an own-type null set; Fubini supplies the integrated
inequality. No pointwise Borel-representative repair is asserted. The measurable origin trace is justified in Appendix~\ref{app:stream}.
Residual functions are kept pointwise, not identified modulo area-null sets.

\begin{theorem}[Certified revenue bracket]\label{thm:bracket}
The complete mechanism in Proposition~\ref{prop:v5-feasible} has exact
revenue $R_\tau$ given by \eqref{eq:v5-revenue}, with
\[
 0.876514341027<R_\tau<0.876514355425.
\]
For the unrestricted randomized class,
\[
 R_\tau\le\OPT\le U<0.882351585489,\qquad
 U-R_\tau<0.005837245<\frac1{100},
\]
where the rational $U$ is specified by \eqref{eq:v5-upper}. Moreover,
\[
 R_\tau/\OPT\ge R_\tau/U>0.993384.
\]
The mechanism therefore earns more than $99.3384\%$ of unrestricted
optimal revenue. Its revenue enclosure has width less than $1.44\times10^{-8}$.
Full outward-rounded endpoints appear in Appendix~\ref{app:reserve-parameter};
the exact revenue is defined by the face integrals, not a decimal endpoint.
This paper neither identifies the optimal revenue nor specifies an optimal mechanism.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Revenue fields and the certificate components}
For each bidder, the integrable radial field
\[
 F^0(x,y)=\frac{3r^2-1}{2r^2}(x,y),\qquad r=\max(x,y),
 \quad F^0(0,0)=0,
\]
and the divergence-free tangent correction $G_i$ define
$\phi_i(v)=F^0(v_i)+G_i(v)$. The rational witness $G_i$ is specified in
Appendix~\ref{app:stream}. The resulting conditional identity is
\[
 R_i(v_{-i})=\int_{[0,1]^2}\phi_i(v_i;v_{-i})\cdot a_i(v_i;v_{-i})\,dv_i
               -u_i(0;v_{-i}).
\]
Appendix~\ref{app:stream} proves the identity for Lipschitz convex utilities
and specifies every rational stream coefficient. A second nonnegative field
$\Psi$ satisfies $\langle\Psi,a\rangle-R=3\int_{D_0}u\ge0$ for
every conditional DSIC/IR mechanism. Its formula, no-sale region $D_0$ and
the square $W_0=[0,43/100]^2$ are given in Appendix~\ref{app:integration}.

Write $B$ for the integral of the stream maximum, $B_{20}$ for its certified
Bernstein majorant and $D$ for the reduction obtained by switching to $\Psi$
on opposing reports in $W_0$. The exact lower bound $\Delta_{1024}\le D$
and four compatible incentive cycles define the reference accounting constant
\[
 U_{\rm ref}=B_{20}-\Delta_{1024}-81/10^{10}.
\]
This constant is used only to assemble the final upper $U$. The certificate
below includes all support replacements and all incentive corrections at once.

\section{Reproducibility and trusted computations}\label{sec:reproduction}
The certificate package specifies the full continuous mechanism, all rational
coefficients and the exact component assembly. Discovery optimizations are
not proof inputs. Polynomial cells are accepted only after inequalities are
certified over their whole domains; finite report checks serve implementation
validation and do not establish continuous incentive compatibility.

The mechanism's DSIC, IR and feasibility follow from the all-real arguments
in Section~\ref{sec:mechanism} and Appendix~\ref{app:compatibility}. Its seed
revenue is independently reconstructed by polynomial/simplex integration,
polygon boundary integrals and vertical slicing. A separate implementation,
importing no source calculator, reconstructs every face enclosure used by the
reserve formula. These calculations share the analytic menu specification.

The upper verification reconstructs the stream polynomials and complete
Bernstein coverage. The $B_{20}$ majorant has two complete implementations.
The $\Delta_{1024}$ subtraction has one full accumulation, with a separate
reconstruction of averaged coefficients, classifications through size 64 and
selected cell integrals. The master system has a separate complete replay
of its shared cells and aggregate bounds. The opposing-excess tree has a full
directed-arithmetic replay and bounded independent cross-checks, rather than
a second independently implemented full tree.

Rational polynomial arithmetic, integer accumulation and directed error
bounds support the numerical enclosures. The opposing-excess traversal also
assumes IEEE binary64 round-to-nearest arithmetic and outward expansion by
NumPy's \texttt{nextafter}. The trusted components are the written analytic
arguments, the stated arithmetic semantics and the verification programs.
This is a computer-assisted proof package, not a proof-assistant formalization.

The package README maps the manuscript's mathematical components to their
machine-readable files and gives a single reproduction command. It checks
the certificate dependencies, exact endpoint assembly, manuscript consistency,
clean compilation and complete SHA-256 coverage. Existing source directory
names are implementation identifiers; they do not specify an additional
auction or change the final theorem. Hashes establish file identity,
separately from analytic validity and computational replay.

\section{Discussion and open problem}
An explicit jointly feasible mechanism earns more than $99.3384\%$ of optimal
revenue, and its certified gap to an unrestricted upper is below $0.005837245$.
The construction prices both physical scarcity and the incentive restrictions
that accompany a change in capacity. The remaining gap is mathematical;
it is not a sampling error or an optimization tolerance.

The reserve transformation provides a globally admissible revenue variation.
Its validity includes exceptional reports and ties, and its revenue includes
the positive-probability faces created by clamping. The positive derivative
at the seed and the separately certified finite gain show what this variation
accomplishes. Neither result proves optimality of the chosen reserve or of
the transformed mechanism.

On the upper side, opposing-field charges and shared-cell aggregation make
conditional replacements and incentive flows compatible in one common price.
Zero charged screening values alone do not supply an equality auction: the
same allocation must also use priced capacity and satisfy the virtual and
incentive equalities. Numerical enclosure remainders are kept separate from
these mechanism slacks.

The reusable content consists of the incentive-preserving transformation,
exact face accounting and common-price compatibility arguments under their
stated hypotheses. The quantitative mechanism and certificate are specific
to the uniform two-bidder, two-item instance. Convex utility, transport duality
and complementary slackness are established principles; no first-in-literature
or cross-instance performance claim is made. The rounded external GemNet
benchmark does not resolve the comparison with its unrounded revenue.

The optimal mechanism and a matching global upper bound remain open.
Stationarity in a restricted family and failure to find a certificate in
one representation do not establish unrestricted optimality.
